01 — The problem

15+ vendor formats.
36 days a year.
And that was one report.
All of it by hand.

For six years I ran procurement for a supplier to TSMC, UMC, PSMC, and Micron, and kept meeting the same thing: work that ran on somebody’s attention, with a fix that looked obvious. So I built what I could — a shipment tracker, a capacity model that carried $6M of imports through the pandemic, and with a colleague, a tool that collapsed the monthly reconciliation. They worked. They were also the limit of what I could build without knowing how to design a database or run anything in the cloud. That limit is why I went back to school.

02 — The work

Where friction becomes software.

ERP Data Integration Tool

Mar 2023 - Dec 2023 · collaborative project · deployed internally

3 days → under 1 hour monthly consolidation

Problem
Inventory analysis meant pulling 8+ report types out of our ERP — purchasing, sales, stock movement — each with a different layout, covering 1,000+ SKUs. Consolidating them by hand was the bottleneck before any analysis could start.
Built
A Python app that auto-detects the header row in inconsistent Excel layouts and maps raw columns to 15 canonical fields by regex, and drops everything else — one sheet with just the items and quantities needed.
Decision
Ingestion, transformation, and validation are separate layers, so onboarding a new ERP format means adding a regex — not touching the pipeline.

Shipment & Stock Tracker

Supertung Industrial · internal tool

asking around → 40% fewer status inquiries one view everyone already knew how to read

Problem
Anyone who needed to know where a shipment was, or how much stock was uncommitted, asked a person. Different people held different numbers, and the discrepancies only surfaced when an order was already promised.
Built
An Apps Script-backed Google Sheets tracker that pulls ERP inventory and inbound shipment data on a refresh schedule, then runs lookup logic and stock allocation calculations against it.

Google Apps ScriptGoogle SheetsERP data

RFID Inventory Service

Aug 2017 - Jan 2018 · Brantabee Italia

2% duplicates → near zero duplicate order rate

Problem
RFID scans drove reordering across 200+ smart wine dispensers, and repeated scans were turning into repeated orders.
Built
A Spring Boot REST service mapping scan events to inventory state transitions in PostgreSQL.
Decision
Made the reorder APIs idempotent by enforcing uniqueness constraints at the database level, so a repeated scan resolves to one order instead of being cleaned up afterwards.

JavaSpring BootPostgreSQL

FlexLab Event Board

Feb 2026 - Jun 2026 · Northeastern FlexLab

clipboard and word of mouth → one screen sign-ups, tool loans, and the questions staff kept answering

Problem
Workshop sign-ups were manual, the tool-loan log lived on a clipboard, and the same few questions went to whoever was standing nearby.
Built
A touchscreen kiosk with four modes — workshop and training listings with in-app registration, a month calendar, and tool borrowing and returns — backed by Supabase over REST.
Decision
Availability is derived from open loan records rather than stored as a counter. A counter drifts permanently the first time a write fails, and nothing surfaces the error.

Topic Voting Platform

Jan 2026 - Apr 2026 · 3-person team

logic in the views → 1 controller, 126 tests business rules testable without the UI

Problem
A JavaFX app where six screens all read and mutate the same voting data. Left in the views, that logic is untestable and behaviour drifts per screen.
Built
Every state change routes through a single AppController, which depends on four repository interfaces rather than concrete storage. In-memory implementations back the app today; an HTTP client built on the JDK’s java.net.http can point the same controller at a REST backend.
Decision
Because no business rule lives in a view, all of it runs headless — 126 JUnit tests across 13 files, 39 of them on the controller alone.
